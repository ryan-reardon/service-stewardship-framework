# Financial Stewardship Playbook

## Engineering Manager Guidance

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Domain Purpose

Maintain transparent, defensible cost management and ensure investment in the service remains justified by the value it delivers.

Financial Stewardship is the discipline of understanding what the service costs, why it costs that, and whether that cost is still proportionate to the value produced. It is distinct from Strategic Stewardship's value model in that it focuses on the operational discipline of cost visibility and management, not just the strategic case for investment.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. What Success Looks Like (Level 3-4 Target State)

A financially well-governed service demonstrates:

- Total cost of ownership (TCO) tracked and understood across infrastructure, licensing, labor, and vendor costs
- Cost attributed to value drivers through an explicit cost-to-value model
- Budget variance monitored and explained, not just reported after the fact
- Licensing and vendor contracts actively managed, including renewal timing, utilization, and negotiation
- Cost optimization treated as continuous work rather than a reaction to budget pressure
- Investment decisions (build, buy, or retire) made with real financial rigor

At Level 4 maturity:

- Cost forecasting informs roadmap and capacity planning ahead of need
- Unit economics are understood and actively improved over time
- Financial posture is modeled under different growth and contraction scenarios

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Engineering Manager Responsibilities

- Maintain visibility into TCO and the major cost drivers behind it
- Own the cost-to-value narrative that justifies continued investment in the service
- Track and manage licensing and vendor contracts, including renewal timelines
- Identify and execute cost optimization opportunities without compromising reliability
- Partner with Finance on budget planning and variance explanation
- Ensure new initiatives include a cost impact assessment in their business case
- Flag financial risk, particularly cost growth that is outpacing delivered value, before it becomes a crisis

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Governance Model & Cadence

**Weekly:**
- Monitor for cost anomalies and unexpected spend spikes

**Monthly:**
- Review TCO trend
- Review budget variance
- Review cost-to-value ratio

**Quarterly:**
- Review vendor and licensing contracts
- Review the cost optimization initiative backlog

**Annually:**
- Conduct a full financial posture review tied to the Strategic Stewardship lifecycle review
- Participate in the annual budget planning cycle

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Required Artifacts

- TCO breakdown by category (infrastructure, licensing, labor, vendor)
- Cost-to-value model
- Budget vs. actual tracking with documented variance explanations
- Vendor and licensing contract inventory with renewal dates and utilization
- Cost optimization backlog
- Financial posture summary showing trend over time

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Operating Mechanisms

- Cost tagging and attribution so spend can be tied to specific services and initiatives
- Budget variance review process with documented explanations, not just numbers
- Vendor and license utilization review to catch underused or redundant tools
- Cost anomaly alerting so spend spikes trigger investigation quickly
- Business case requirement including cost impact for new initiatives above a defined threshold

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Common Failure Modes (Anti-Patterns)

- Cost only reviewed once a year during budget season
- No one on the team can explain why the service costs what it costs
- Licenses and vendor contracts auto-renew without a utilization review
- Cost optimization treated as a one-time project instead of a continuous discipline
- Financial posture disconnected from the value the service actually delivers
- New initiatives approved without any cost impact assessment

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Maturity Advancement Roadmap

Level 1 → Reactive:
- Establish basic TCO visibility
- Inventory vendor and licensing contracts
- Identify the major cost drivers

Level 2 → Managed:
- Implement budget vs. actual tracking with variance review
- Establish a cost-to-value model, even if rough
- Set a renewal review process for contracts

Level 3 → Optimized:
- Institutionalize a continuous cost optimization backlog
- Tie business cases to cost impact for new initiatives
- Improve cost attribution granularity

Level 4 → Proactive:
- Forecast cost under growth and contraction scenarios
- Track and improve unit economics
- Integrate financial posture into strategic roadmap planning

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. Monthly Metrics Dashboard

- TCO trend, total and by category
- Budget vs. actual variance
- Cost-to-value ratio trend
- Vendor and license utilization rate
- Cost optimization savings realized, cumulative
- Cost per unit (transaction, user, or request, where applicable)
- Upcoming contract renewals and risk flags

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. 90-Day Action Plan for a New Manager

**Days 1-30:**
- Inventory current TCO and the major cost drivers
- Review vendor and licensing contracts and upcoming renewals
- Review budget vs. actual history and identify unexplained variance
- Assess whether a cost-to-value model exists; if not, begin drafting one
- Identify obvious cost optimization opportunities

**Days 31-60:**
- Implement or refine the budget variance review process
- Establish cost tagging and attribution if it doesn't already exist
- Launch the top cost optimization initiatives
- Renegotiate or right-size underutilized vendor and license agreements
- Draft the cost-to-value narrative for stakeholders

**Days 61-90:**
- Publish the financial posture summary and review cadence
- Formalize the business case requirement for new initiatives
- Set measurable cost optimization targets
- Establish a quarterly vendor and contract review forum
- Connect financial posture reporting to the Strategic Stewardship lifecycle review
