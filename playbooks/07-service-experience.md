# Service Experience Playbook

## Engineering Manager Guidance

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Domain Purpose

Ensure the service delivers a consistently positive experience for the people who use it, that expectations are clearly set and met, and that feedback actively shapes the roadmap.

Service Experience is the discipline of understanding how customers and users actually experience the service, not just whether it is up. A service can be reliable by every internal metric and still be failing the people who depend on it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. What Success Looks Like (Level 3-4 Target State)

A service with strong experience management demonstrates:

- Clearly defined customer/user segments with documented expectations (usability, response time, support responsiveness)
- Consistent measurement of satisfaction (CSAT, NPS, or an equivalent proxy)
- Support and success functions operate proactively, not only in response to tickets
- Documentation and self-service reduce friction and ticket volume
- Feedback loops feed directly into roadmap prioritization, with a visible connection between what users say and what gets built
- Communication during incidents and changes is clear, timely, and proactive rather than defensive

At Level 4 maturity:

- Experience metrics are predictive, surfacing dissatisfaction or churn risk before it shows up in support volume
- Experience is segmented and personalized based on how different user groups actually use the service
- Self-service and deflection continuously improve through usage analytics rather than periodic redesigns

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Engineering Manager Responsibilities

- Define and track satisfaction metrics for the service's actual users, not just uptime
- Own the end-to-end experience: onboarding, day-to-day usage, support, and communication
- Ensure documentation and self-service resources stay current as the service changes
- Build feedback channels and ensure feedback is visibly connected to roadmap decisions
- Set and enforce communication standards for incidents and major changes
- Partner with Support, Success, and Product on recurring friction points and escalation trends
- Reduce support ticket volume through root-cause fixes rather than repeated triage

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Governance Model & Cadence

**Weekly:**
- Review support ticket trends and active escalations

**Monthly:**
- Review satisfaction metrics (CSAT/NPS/ticket sentiment)
- Review documentation gaps and self-service effectiveness

**Quarterly:**
- Conduct an experience review with Support, Success, and Product
- Reassess customer/user segments and whether documented expectations still hold

**Annually:**
- Refresh the customer/user journey map
- Validate experience priorities against the strategic roadmap (Strategic Stewardship)

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Required Artifacts

- Customer/user segment definitions and journey map
- Documented service expectations (usability, responsiveness, support SLAs)
- Satisfaction metrics dashboard (CSAT/NPS/ticket sentiment)
- Support ticket taxonomy with root-cause tracking
- Documentation and self-service inventory
- Communication protocol for incidents and major changes
- Feedback intake and prioritization process

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Operating Mechanisms

- Feedback intake mechanism (surveys, ticket sentiment, direct interviews) that feeds the backlog rather than a drawer
- Documentation review cadence tied to release cycles, not a separate forgotten task
- Standing communication templates for incidents and major changes
- Root-cause tracking for recurring support issues, tied into Engineering Excellence and Operational Reliability backlogs
- Self-service and deflection measurement with a defined improvement loop

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Common Failure Modes (Anti-Patterns)

- Experience measured only through incident count, never through lived user experience
- Documentation stale and disconnected from current product behavior
- Feedback collected through surveys or tickets but never actioned or connected to roadmap
- Incident communication reactive and inconsistent, eroding trust over time
- Support treated purely as a cost center to minimize rather than a signal source
- No clear, current definition of who the customer or user actually is

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Maturity Advancement Roadmap

Level 1 → Reactive:
- Identify primary customer/user segments
- Establish basic support ticket intake and tracking
- Create an initial incident communication template

Level 2 → Managed:
- Implement satisfaction measurement (CSAT/NPS)
- Build a documentation and onboarding baseline
- Establish root-cause tracking for the top recurring support issues

Level 3 → Optimized:
- Connect feedback loops directly to roadmap prioritization
- Formalize communication protocols for incidents and changes
- Reduce support ticket volume through systemic fixes rather than repeated triage

Level 4 → Proactive:
- Predict satisfaction and churn risk from usage and support signals
- Personalize experience based on segment and usage patterns
- Continuously improve self-service through usage analytics

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. Monthly Metrics Dashboard

- CSAT / NPS trend
- Support ticket volume and resolution time
- Ticket root-cause distribution (recurring vs. novel)
- Self-service deflection rate
- Documentation coverage and freshness
- Time to first response / time to resolution
- Incident and change communication timeliness
- Feedback-to-roadmap conversion rate

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. 90-Day Action Plan for a New Manager

**Days 1-30:**
- Identify customer/user segments and their primary use cases
- Audit existing satisfaction measurement, if any
- Review support ticket volume, trends, and root causes
- Review documentation and self-service resources for gaps
- Review the history and quality of incident/change communication
- Meet with Support, Success, and Product to understand recurring pain points

**Days 31-60:**
- Implement or refine a satisfaction measurement mechanism
- Establish root-cause tracking and connect top issues to the engineering backlog
- Refresh key documentation and close the highest-impact self-service gaps
- Draft standardized communication templates for incidents and changes
- Begin a feedback intake process that feeds roadmap discussions

**Days 61-90:**
- Publish an experience scorecard and review cadence
- Close out the highest-impact recurring support issues
- Formalize the feedback-to-roadmap process with Product
- Establish a quarterly experience review forum
- Set measurable targets for satisfaction, ticket volume, and deflection rate
