# Value Execution Assessment

This assessment evaluates the maturity of Value Execution within a technology service.

The objective is delivery honesty, not optimistic scoring.

Score conservatively.  
Require evidence.  
Default to the lower level when uncertain.

---

# Domain Purpose

Value Execution ensures the service delivers measurable business outcomes predictably, efficiently, and sustainably.

Busy teams are not the same as effective teams.  
Outcome accountability is what distinguishes the two.

---

# Level 1: Reactive

Delivery is informal and outcome-blind.

Characteristics:

- No explicit outcome definitions tied to initiatives
- Backlog prioritization based on recency, noise, or stakeholder pressure
- Commitments made without a capacity model
- WIP unmanaged; many things in-flight simultaneously
- Delivery cadence unpredictable
- Retrospectives absent or produce no tracked changes
- Work shipped without consistent operational readiness

Evidence Review:

- Do initiatives have documented success metrics before work starts?
- Is there a prioritization model beyond "what's most urgent"?
- Is capacity explicitly tracked and allocated?
- Are WIP limits in place?
- Are retrospective actions owned and tracked?

If most answers are "no," score Level 1.

---

# Level 2: Managed

Basic delivery structure exists, but discipline is inconsistent.

Characteristics:

- Backlog exists with basic prioritization
- Some initiatives have outcome definitions
- Planning cadence established but not always followed
- Retrospectives occur but rarely produce systemic changes
- Capacity planning is implicit rather than explicit
- Cross-team dependencies tracked informally
- Operational readiness included inconsistently in "done"

Evidence Review:

- Backlog is groomed and roughly ordered by value or priority
- Planning cadence is documented and followed most of the time
- Some outcome definitions exist for major initiatives
- Retrospective log exists, even if action tracking is weak
- Delivery metrics are tracked at a basic level

If delivery structure exists but does not consistently produce predictable outcomes, score Level 2.

---

# Level 3: Optimized

Delivery is disciplined, outcome-oriented, and flow-aware.

Characteristics:

- Explicit outcome definitions required before work begins
- Backlog prioritized using a defined value and risk scoring model
- Capacity allocation model enforced (run / grow / modernize / security)
- WIP limits enforced; parallel initiatives managed deliberately
- Delivery predictability tracked and actively improved
- Cross-team dependencies owned and managed with explicit dates and risks
- Retrospective actions tracked, owned, and completed
- Operational readiness embedded in definition of done
- Trade-offs documented and reviewed with stakeholders

Evidence Review:

- Outcome definitions present for active and recent initiatives
- Value scoring or equivalent prioritization criteria documented and applied
- Capacity model published and used in planning
- WIP and queue health reviewed regularly
- Delivery metrics tracked: lead time, cycle time, throughput, predictability
- Dependency log maintained with owners and risk status
- Retrospective action log exists with completion tracking

If delivery discipline measurably improves predictability and outcome realization, score Level 3.

---

# Level 4: Proactive

Delivery is forecasted, continuously calibrated, and outcome-validated.

Characteristics:

- Delivery forecasting updated continuously based on flow data
- Capacity planning is scenario-based (growth, incidents, org changes)
- Post-delivery outcome measurement is routine and feeds back into prioritization
- Outcome data drives what gets built next
- Value realization tracking is embedded in operational governance
- Trade-off decisions are evidence-driven and documented
- Bottleneck removal is systematic and measurably improves throughput

Evidence Review:

- Forecast accuracy tracked over time
- Scenario-based capacity plans in use
- Post-delivery outcome measurement documented for completed initiatives
- Prioritization decisions visibly informed by outcome data
- Flow bottlenecks tracked and systematically resolved

If delivery decisions are anticipatory and continuously refined by outcome evidence, score Level 4.

---

# Scoring Summary

| Level | Posture |
|-------|---------|
| 1 | Reactive and outcome-blind |
| 2 | Structured but inconsistent |
| 3 | Disciplined and flow-managed |
| 4 | Predictive and outcome-validated |

---

# Risk Considerations

Value Execution weaknesses often manifest as:

- Teams shipping features that don't move business metrics
- Delivery commitments chronically missed or renegotiated
- High throughput masking low value realization
- Reliability and modernization perpetually deprioritized
- Stakeholder confidence eroding despite visible team busyness
- Incident load and support toil consuming capacity invisibly

Low maturity in this domain creates strategic waste: effort expended without proportional outcome.

Execution dysfunction is often normalized gradually and becomes invisible until outcomes are audited.

---

# Target Level & Improvement Plan

After scoring:

1. Document current level.
2. Define target level (typically +1 from current).
3. Identify the delivery discipline gaps with greatest impact.
4. Assign accountable owner.
5. Define timeline for reassessment.

Reassess quarterly during active improvement periods.

---

# Final Reminder

A team can be consistently busy while consistently failing to deliver measurable outcomes.
Value Execution determines whether effort converts into impact.
Score honestly.
