# Service Experience Assessment

This assessment evaluates the maturity of Service Experience within a technology service.

The objective is customer trust integrity, not internal process optimism.

Score conservatively.  
Require evidence.  
Default to the lower level when uncertain.

---

# Domain Purpose

Service Experience ensures customers and consumers receive a predictable, transparent, and professional experience across every interaction with the service.

A technically reliable service can still fail its customers.  
Experience is what customers remember. Reliability is the baseline they expect.

---

# Level 1: Reactive

Support is ad hoc and customer experience is undefined.

Characteristics:

- No documented support model; customers unsure how to get help
- Tickets lack structure; triage is inconsistent and time-consuming
- Response and resolution times unmeasured or unmeasurable
- Communication during incidents informal and inconsistent
- Knowledge base absent or severely outdated
- Recurring issues handled repeatedly without root cause investment
- Support load impact on engineering capacity unmeasured

Evidence Review:

- Is a support model documented and published to customers?
- Are response and resolution targets defined?
- Is ticket intake structured with severity classification?
- Are incident communications timely and consistent?
- Does a knowledge base exist for top issues?
- Are repeat issues tracked and root cause work prioritized?

If most answers are "no," score Level 1.

---

# Level 2: Managed

Basic support structure exists but consistency and quality are limited.

Characteristics:

- Support channel and ownership defined and communicated
- Basic ticket tracking in place; severity classification informal
- Response targets defined but compliance inconsistently tracked
- Incident communication occurs but cadence and format vary
- Knowledge base exists for top issues; maintenance uneven
- Repeat issue register introduced; root cause work beginning
- Escalation paths documented but not always followed

Evidence Review:

- Support model documentation exists and is findable by customers
- Ticket taxonomy defined with basic categories and severity levels
- Response time tracking in place for primary ticket classes
- Communication templates exist for incident updates
- Knowledge base covers most common issues
- Repeat issue register exists with at least some root cause tracking

If support structure exists but does not consistently meet customer expectations, score Level 2.

---

# Level 3: Optimized

Support is governed, measured, and producing measurable trust improvements.

Characteristics:

- Support model fully documented: channels, hours, ownership, escalation paths, and severity definitions
- Response and resolution targets defined, tracked, and met consistently
- Intake structured with required fields, classification rules, and routing logic
- Incident communication timely and consistent with defined cadence and single source of truth
- Knowledge base actively maintained with deflection rate measured
- Repeat issues systematically reduced through root cause engineering investment
- Customer feedback systematically captured and translated into roadmap items
- Support load impact on engineering capacity tracked and managed

Evidence Review:

- Support model published and referenced by customers
- Response and resolution SLA compliance tracked with trend data
- Ticket intake quality metric tracked: percentage with complete information
- Communication templates in use with post-incident summaries published
- Knowledge base deflection rate measured and improving
- Repeat issue register shows reduction trend
- Customer feedback present in backlog or roadmap planning artifacts

If support governance measurably reduces repeat issues and improves customer satisfaction, score Level 3.

---

# Level 4: Proactive

Support demand is forecasted, proactively reduced, and increasingly automated.

Characteristics:

- Support demand forecasted and proactive reduction measures taken before volume increases
- Automated resolution handling common request classes without engineer involvement
- Proactive communications preventing avoidable escalations
- CSAT trend continuously measured and improvement targets actively pursued
- Self-service capability mature enough to handle majority of routine requests
- Engineering investment in experience improvement driven by data, not escalation pressure

Evidence Review:

- Demand forecasting evidence: volume trends reviewed and acted on proactively
- Automation in place for at least one class of common requests
- Proactive communication program documented with outreach cadence
- CSAT trend data reviewed in quarterly governance cadence
- Self-service deflection rate high and improving
- Experience improvement roadmap tied to customer feedback and trend data

If support experience is anticipatory and satisfaction is continuously improving, score Level 4.

---

# Scoring Summary

| Level | Posture |
|-------|---------|
| 1 | Ad hoc and undefined |
| 2 | Structured but inconsistently delivered |
| 3 | Governed and measurably improving |
| 4 | Proactive and increasingly automated |

---

# Risk Considerations

Service Experience weaknesses often manifest as:

- Customer escalations that bypass process and consume disproportionate engineering time
- Recurring incidents generating repeated support volume with no engineering response
- Communication gaps during outages eroding customer confidence faster than the outage itself
- Knowledge base debt increasing support load as the service grows
- Support burden silently consuming engineering capacity that does not appear in planning
- Customer churn or dissatisfaction driven by experience rather than reliability

Low maturity in this domain creates customer trust risk that technical excellence alone cannot recover.

Support dysfunction is often invisible inside the engineering team and highly visible to every customer who encounters it.

---

# Target Level & Improvement Plan

After scoring:

1. Document current level.
2. Define target level aligned to customer segment expectations and service criticality.
3. Identify the experience gaps with greatest customer trust impact.
4. Assign accountable owner.
5. Define timeline for reassessment.

Reassess quarterly during active improvement periods.

---

# Final Reminder

Customers do not experience architecture. They experience support, communication, and consistency. A service can be technically excellent and still fail its customers at the experience layer. Score honestly.
