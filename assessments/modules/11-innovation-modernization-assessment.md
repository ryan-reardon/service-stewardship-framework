# Innovation & Modernization Assessment

This assessment evaluates the maturity of Innovation & Modernization within a technology service.

The objective is legacy risk reduction and modernization delivery, not technology novelty.

Score conservatively.  
Require evidence.  
Default to the lower level when uncertain.

---

# Domain Purpose

Innovation & Modernization ensures the service evolves deliberately, replacing aging technology and practices before they become a source of risk, and that experimentation capacity is protected rather than perpetually deferred.

Modernization capacity that is never protected is never delivered.
Legacy risk that is never measured accumulates silently until it becomes urgent.

---

# Level 1: Reactive

Modernization is unplanned and legacy risk is invisible until it surfaces through incidents or delivery blockers.

Characteristics:

- No inventory of legacy or aging components
- No modernization capacity allocation or protection in planning
- Experimentation informal, invisible, and capacity-constrained
- Technology adoption driven by individual preference without evaluation criteria
- No technology radar or equivalent
- Modernization work invisible to stakeholders

Evidence Review:

- Does a legacy and aging component inventory exist with risk ratings?
- Is modernization capacity explicitly allocated and protected in planning?
- Is there a structured experimentation process with time-boxing and evaluation criteria?
- Does a technology radar or equivalent exist?
- Is modernization progress reported to stakeholders?

If most answers are "no," score Level 1.

---

# Level 2: Managed

Basic modernization structure exists but protection, prioritization, and experimentation governance are limited.

Characteristics:

- Legacy component inventory started with initial risk identification
- Modernization capacity discussed in planning but not consistently protected
- Prioritization criteria introduced for modernization backlog
- Experimentation occurs but without a formal process or time-boxing
- Initial technology radar created, though not regularly maintained
- Some modernization progress communicated informally

Evidence Review:

- Legacy inventory exists with at least the highest-risk components identified
- Modernization capacity appears in planning, even if inconsistently protected
- Modernization backlog maintained with at least informal prioritization
- Technology radar exists with documented positions
- At least one modernization initiative completed in the past two quarters

If modernization structure exists but capacity is frequently consumed by delivery pressure, score Level 2.

---

# Level 3: Optimized

Modernization is explicitly protected, prioritized by risk, and tracked and communicated to stakeholders.

Characteristics:

- Legacy and aging component inventory current with risk ratings and retirement plans
- Modernization capacity allocation protected in planning and enforced against delivery pressure
- Modernization backlog maintained with documented risk-based prioritization criteria
- Experimentation process formalized with time-boxing, evaluation criteria, and adopt/stop decision gates
- Technology radar reviewed quarterly with documented rationale for position changes
- Modernization progress reported regularly to stakeholders
- Modernization priorities connected to architectural debt register and strategic goals

Evidence Review:

- Legacy inventory shows risk trend and retirement plans for top-risk components
- Modernization capacity allocation tracked with planned versus actual comparison
- Experimentation log present with outcomes documented
- Technology radar updated within the last quarter with change rationale
- Modernization progress report shared with stakeholders in the last quarter
- Debt register cross-referenced to modernization backlog

If modernization governance measurably reduces legacy risk and delivers planned modernization work, score Level 3.

---

# Level 4: Proactive

Modernization is predictive, experimentation feeds the roadmap systematically, and platform evolution is industry-informed.

Characteristics:

- Modernization needs predicted with aging risk modeled before becoming urgent
- Experimentation outcomes systematically feeding roadmap decisions rather than dead-ending after proofs of concept
- Platform evolution decisions informed by ongoing industry and technology trend analysis
- Legacy risk trend declining on a measurable trajectory
- Technology radar changes data-driven rather than opinion-driven

Evidence Review:

- Aging risk modeling present and referenced in planning discussions
- Experimentation-to-roadmap conversion rate tracked and positive
- Industry and trend analysis inputs documented in technology radar rationale
- Legacy component count and risk trend declining over time
- Modernization roadmap commitments met at a high rate

If modernization decisions are anticipatory and experimentation outcomes reliably feed the roadmap, score Level 4.

---

# Scoring Summary

| Level | Posture |
|-------|---------|
| 1 | Unplanned and risk-invisible |
| 2 | Structured but inconsistently protected |
| 3 | Protected, prioritized, and communicated |
| 4 | Predictive and systematically self-improving |

---

# Risk Considerations

Innovation & Modernization weaknesses often manifest as:

- Legacy components causing recurring incidents that modernization would have prevented
- Feature delivery blocked or slowed by legacy constraints that accumulated without visibility
- Experimentation producing proofs of concept that never become production decisions
- Technology adoption creating sprawl because no evaluation criteria or radar exist
- Modernization invisible to stakeholders and defunded as a result
- End-of-life components remaining in production because no retirement plan was ever created

Low maturity in this domain creates compounding risk across Operational Reliability, Security & Risk Governance, and Architectural Integrity.

Services that never modernize eventually cannot modernize because the cost and risk of change exceed the appetite to fund it.

---

# Target Level & Improvement Plan

After scoring:

1. Document current level.
2. Define target level aligned to the service's lifecycle stage and legacy risk exposure.
3. Identify the modernization governance gaps with greatest risk or delivery impact.
4. Assign accountable owner.
5. Define timeline for reassessment.

Reassess quarterly during active improvement periods.

---

# Final Reminder

A service can appear stable while accumulating legacy risk that will eventually make change impossible. Modernization capacity that is always deferred is never delivered. Score honestly.
