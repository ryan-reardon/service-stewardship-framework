# Service Experience

Service Experience ensures the service delivers a consistently positive experience for the people who use it, that expectations are clearly set and met, and that feedback actively shapes the roadmap.

It is the discipline of continuously validating:

- Do customers and users experience the service as reliable and usable, not just internally measured as up?
- Are satisfaction metrics tracked and connected to real decisions?
- Does feedback reach the roadmap, or does it disappear into a drawer?
- Is communication during disruptions timely, proactive, and trust-preserving?

Service Experience transforms the service from a system that performs internally into one that earns trust externally.

---

# 1. Domain Purpose

To ensure that:

- Customer and user segments are defined, with documented expectations for usability, responsiveness, and support.
- Satisfaction is measured consistently and used to inform decisions, not just reported.
- Documentation and self-service resources reduce friction and stay current as the service evolves.
- Feedback is captured through structured channels and visibly connected to roadmap decisions.
- Communication during incidents and major changes is proactive, consistent, and trust-building.
- Support is treated as a signal source, not only a cost center.

Without Service Experience, a service can be reliable by every internal metric while still failing the people who depend on it.

---

# 2. Target State (Level 3–4 Characteristics)

A service with strong experience management demonstrates:

- Clearly defined customer and user segments with documented expectations for usability, response time, and support responsiveness
- Consistent satisfaction measurement through CSAT, NPS, or an equivalent proxy reviewed on a defined cadence
- Support and success functions operating proactively, not only in response to escalations
- Documentation and self-service that reduce friction and ticket volume, maintained as the service changes
- Feedback loops that feed directly into roadmap prioritization with a visible connection between what users say and what gets built
- Communication during incidents and changes that is clear, timely, and proactive rather than reactive and defensive

At higher maturity levels:

- Experience metrics are predictive, surfacing dissatisfaction or churn risk before it appears in support volume
- Experience is segmented based on how different user groups actually use the service
- Self-service and deflection improve continuously through usage analytics rather than periodic redesigns

---

# 3. Core Responsibilities

Service Experience requires the Engineering Manager or Service Owner to:

- Define and track satisfaction metrics for the service's actual users, not just uptime or internal SLOs
- Own the end-to-end experience: onboarding, day-to-day usage, support, and communication
- Ensure documentation and self-service resources stay current as the service changes
- Build feedback channels and ensure feedback is visibly connected to roadmap decisions
- Set and enforce communication standards for incidents and major changes
- Partner with Support, Success, and Product on recurring friction points and escalation trends
- Reduce support ticket volume through root-cause engineering fixes rather than repeated triage

Ownership is measured by satisfaction trends and feedback-to-roadmap conversion, not incident count alone.

---

# 4. Governance Model & Cadence

Service Experience operates on a defined cadence.

**Weekly**
- Review support ticket trends and active escalations

**Monthly**
- Review satisfaction metrics: CSAT, NPS, or ticket sentiment trends
- Review documentation gaps and self-service effectiveness

**Quarterly**
- Conduct an experience review with Support, Success, and Product
- Reassess customer and user segments and whether documented expectations still hold

**Annually**
- Refresh the customer and user journey map
- Validate experience priorities against the strategic roadmap

Experience governance that only activates at escalation is reactive support, not a managed experience program.

---

# 5. Required Artifacts

The following artifacts must exist and remain current:

- Customer and user segment definitions with journey map
- Documented service expectations covering usability, responsiveness, and support SLAs
- Satisfaction metrics dashboard tracking CSAT, NPS, or ticket sentiment trends
- Support ticket taxonomy with root-cause tracking
- Documentation and self-service inventory with coverage and freshness indicators
- Communication protocol and templates for incidents and major changes
- Feedback intake and prioritization process showing connection to roadmap

Artifacts must reflect the service's actual user segments and current behavior, not its state at launch.

---

# 6. Operating Mechanisms

Service Experience relies on structured mechanisms:

- Feedback intake mechanism through surveys, ticket sentiment, and direct interviews that feeds the backlog rather than a filing system
- Documentation review cadence tied to release cycles, ensuring content stays current as the service evolves
- Standing communication templates for incidents and major changes with defined update cadence
- Root-cause tracking for recurring support issues tied to Engineering Excellence and Operational Reliability backlogs
- Self-service and deflection measurement with a defined improvement loop reviewed monthly

These mechanisms convert customer signals into engineering and roadmap action.

---

# 7. Key Metrics

Service Experience must be measurable.

Representative metrics include:

- CSAT and NPS trend over time
- Support ticket volume and resolution time
- Ticket root-cause distribution: recurring versus novel issues
- Self-service deflection rate
- Documentation coverage and freshness indicators
- Time to first response and time to resolution by severity
- Incident and change communication timeliness
- Feedback-to-roadmap conversion rate

Metrics should drive root-cause investment and self-service improvement, not just SLA compliance.

---

# 8. Leading Risk Indicators

Watch for:

- Satisfaction measured only through incident count, never through lived user experience
- Documentation stale and disconnected from current service behavior
- Feedback collected but never actioned or visibly connected to the roadmap
- Incident communication reactive and inconsistent, eroding trust over time
- Support treated purely as a cost center to minimize rather than a signal source for engineering
- No clear, current definition of who the customer or user actually is

Support dysfunction is often invisible inside the engineering team and highly visible to every customer who encounters it.

---

# 9. Maturity Progression Model

Service Experience evolves through staged progression:

**Level 1: Reactive**
- Primary customer and user segments identified
- Basic support ticket intake and tracking established
- Initial incident communication template created

**Level 2: Managed**
- Satisfaction measurement implemented through CSAT or NPS
- Documentation and onboarding baseline built
- Root-cause tracking established for top recurring support issues

**Level 3: Optimized**
- Feedback loops connected directly to roadmap prioritization
- Communication protocols formalized for incidents and changes
- Support ticket volume reduced through systemic engineering fixes
- Experience reviewed quarterly with Support, Success, and Product

**Level 4: Predictive**
- Satisfaction and churn risk predicted from usage and support signals
- Experience segmented and personalized based on user group patterns
- Self-service continuously improved through usage analytics

Progression should be intentional and evidence-based.

---

# 10. Relationship to Other Domains

Service Experience depends on:

- Operational Reliability (customers experience reliability directly; SLA attainment is the foundation of support credibility)
- Engineering Excellence (operational readiness at launch prevents the support burden created by missing documentation and unclear ownership)
- Observability & Intelligence (accurate detection and status communication during incidents requires instrumented systems with trustworthy signals)

Service Experience informs:

- Strategic Stewardship (satisfaction trends and churn risk are leading indicators of service relevance)
- Value Execution (recurring support issues surface unmet customer needs that belong in the backlog)
- Financial Stewardship (support cost is a TCO component that self-service investment can measurably reduce)
- Innovation & Modernization (high support load driven by legacy behavior is a modernization signal)

Without Service Experience, technical excellence does not translate into customer trust.

---

# Summary

Service Experience determines whether customers trust the service, not just whether the service works. A service can be reliable by every internal metric while failing the people who depend on it. Durable customer relationships are built on measured satisfaction, honest communication, self-service capability, and a consistent commitment to connecting what users experience to what the team chooses to build next.
