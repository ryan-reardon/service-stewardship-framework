# Innovation & Modernization

Innovation & Modernization ensures the service evolves deliberately, replacing aging technology and practices before they become a source of risk, and that experimentation capacity is protected rather than perpetually deferred.

It is the discipline of continuously validating:

- Are aging and legacy components visible, risk-rated, and on a retirement path?
- Is modernization capacity protected in planning, or does it always lose to feature delivery?
- Is experimentation structured and time-boxed, or informal and invisible?
- Are modernization priorities driven by risk and value, or only by age and complaint?

Innovation & Modernization transforms technical evolution from crisis response into planned, funded work.

---

# 1. Domain Purpose

To ensure that:

- Modernization capacity is explicitly protected in planning, tied to the run/grow/modernize allocation.
- Legacy and deprecated components are identified with concrete retirement or replacement plans.
- Dedicated capacity and a lightweight process exist for experimentation: spikes, proofs of concept, and evaluation of new approaches.
- Modernization initiatives are prioritized by risk and value, not simply by age.
- A technology radar or equivalent tracks what to adopt, trial, hold, or retire.
- Modernization progress is tracked and communicated rather than happening silently in the background.

Without Innovation & Modernization, services that never evolve eventually cannot, because the cost and risk of change grow faster than the appetite to fund them.

---

# 2. Target State (Level 3–4 Characteristics)

A service with mature innovation and modernization practice demonstrates:

- Modernization capacity explicitly protected in planning and enforced against delivery pressure
- Legacy and deprecated components identified with concrete retirement or replacement plans and risk ratings
- Dedicated capacity and a lightweight process for experimentation with time-boxed evaluation criteria
- Modernization initiatives prioritized using explicit risk and value criteria, not only age
- A technology radar or equivalent tracking adopt, trial, hold, and retire decisions
- Modernization progress tracked and communicated to stakeholders rather than invisible background work

At higher maturity levels:

- Modernization needs are predicted with aging risk modeled before it becomes urgent
- Experimentation outcomes systematically feed the roadmap instead of dead-ending after proofs of concept
- Platform evolution decisions are informed by ongoing industry and technology trend analysis

---

# 3. Core Responsibilities

Innovation & Modernization requires the Engineering Manager or Service Owner to:

- Maintain visibility into aging and legacy components and their risk profile
- Protect modernization and experimentation capacity from being consumed by delivery pressure
- Prioritize modernization initiatives using explicit risk and value criteria
- Establish a lightweight process for evaluating and adopting new tools or approaches with time-boxed evaluation
- Track and communicate modernization progress to stakeholders
- Partner with Architectural Integrity on debt-driven modernization priorities
- Avoid both extremes: chasing every new technology without evaluation criteria, and never modernizing at all

Ownership is measured by legacy risk reduction and modernization delivery, not by technology novelty.

---

# 4. Governance Model & Cadence

Innovation & Modernization operates on a defined cadence.

**Weekly**
- Track active modernization and experimentation work in progress

**Monthly**
- Review the modernization backlog and capacity allocation
- Review experimentation outcomes and adoption decisions

**Quarterly**
- Conduct a formal modernization roadmap review
- Refresh the technology radar with adopt, trial, hold, and retire updates

**Annually**
- Conduct strategic modernization planning tied to the Strategic Stewardship lifecycle review
- Review obsolescence risk and component age distribution

If modernization is only discussed annually, it will always lose to quarterly delivery pressure.

---

# 5. Required Artifacts

The following artifacts must exist and remain current:

- Legacy and aging component inventory with risk ratings and retirement plans
- Modernization backlog with documented prioritization criteria and capacity allocation
- Technology radar covering adopt, trial, hold, and retire positions
- Experimentation and proof-of-concept log with outcomes: adopted, rejected, or ongoing
- Modernization capacity allocation record showing planned versus actual
- Modernization progress report for stakeholder communication

Artifacts should be actively used in planning and quarterly reviews, not maintained solely for documentation.

---

# 6. Operating Mechanisms

Innovation & Modernization relies on structured mechanisms:

- Modernization capacity guardrail: an explicit percentage or time allocation protected from delivery pressure with enforcement in planning
- Risk-based prioritization rubric for modernization initiatives using component age, business impact, and retirement cost
- Lightweight experimentation process using time-boxed spikes or proofs of concept with defined evaluation criteria and adopt/stop decision gates
- Technology radar review process on a quarterly cadence with documented rationale for position changes
- Regular modernization progress reporting keeping stakeholders informed and preventing modernization from being dismissed as invisible background work

These mechanisms convert modernization intent into protected, funded, measurable delivery.

---

# 7. Key Metrics

Innovation & Modernization must be measurable.

Representative metrics include:

- Modernization capacity allocated versus planned
- Legacy and aging component count and risk trend over time
- Modernization backlog age and throughput
- Experimentation and proof-of-concept count with outcomes: adopted, rejected, or ongoing
- Technology radar changes per quarter
- Modernization progress versus roadmap commitments

Metrics should surface legacy risk accumulation and modernization delivery, not just activity volume.

---

# 8. Leading Risk Indicators

Watch for:

- Modernization always losing to feature delivery pressure, never appearing in completed work
- Legacy components ignored until they cause an outage or block a feature
- Experimentation happening informally and invisibly with no protected capacity or structured evaluation
- New technology adopted based on individual preference rather than documented evaluation criteria
- Modernization work invisible to stakeholders and dismissed as gold-plating or technical indulgence
- No clear retirement plan for deprecated components, which linger indefinitely and accumulate maintenance burden

Services that never modernize eventually cannot modernize because the cost and risk of change exceed the appetite to fund it.

---

# 9. Maturity Progression Model

Innovation & Modernization evolves through staged progression:

**Level 1: Reactive**
- Legacy and aging component inventory built
- Highest-risk modernization candidates identified
- Minimal experimentation capacity established

**Level 2: Managed**
- Modernization capacity allocation protected and enforced
- Prioritization criteria established for the modernization backlog
- Initial technology radar created

**Level 3: Optimized**
- Experimentation process formalized with evaluation criteria and time-boxing
- Modernization progress tracked and reported to stakeholders regularly
- Modernization priorities tied to architectural debt register and strategic goals

**Level 4: Proactive**
- Modernization needs predicted before they become urgent
- Experimentation outcomes systematically feeding roadmap decisions
- Platform evolution informed by industry and technology trend analysis

Progression should be intentional and evidence-based.

---

# 10. Relationship to Other Domains

Innovation & Modernization depends on:

- Strategic Stewardship (modernization capacity allocation comes from the run/grow/modernize framework and must be defended strategically)
- Architectural Integrity (the architectural debt register is the primary input for debt-driven modernization prioritization)
- Value Execution (modernization capacity must be protected in delivery planning alongside feature work)

Innovation & Modernization enables:

- Operational Reliability (modernization reduces the legacy component risk that drives recurring incident classes)
- Engineering Excellence (adoption of improved engineering practices is a modernization outcome)
- Financial Stewardship (modernization reduces the maintenance burden and cost of legacy component ownership)
- Security & Risk Governance (replacing end-of-life components reduces security exposure before it becomes critical)

Without Innovation & Modernization, services accumulate risk faster than they can manage it.

---

# Summary

Innovation & Modernization determines whether a service can evolve or only endure. Technical debt and legacy risk compound silently until the cost of change exceeds the appetite to fund it. Durable modernization is built on protected capacity, risk-based prioritization, structured experimentation, and a consistent commitment to retiring what the service has outgrown before it becomes a source of risk rather than a candidate for investment.
