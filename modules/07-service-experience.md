# Service Experience

Service Experience ensures customers and consumers of the service receive a predictable, transparent, and professional experience across every interaction.

It is the discipline of continuously validating:

- Do customers know how to get help, and does help arrive as expected?
- Are issues triaged efficiently, routed correctly, and resolved durably?
- Is communication timely and consistent during disruptions?
- Is customer feedback captured and used to improve the service?

Service Experience transforms support from a reactive cost center into a trust-building system.

---

# 1. Domain Purpose

To ensure that:

- A clear support model exists: channels, ownership, escalation paths, and response expectations.
- Issues are triaged effectively and do not bounce between teams.
- Recurring issues are reduced through root cause investment, not repeatedly handled.
- Incident communications are timely, consistent, and come from a single source of truth.
- A knowledge base enables self-service and reduces support load over time.
- Customer feedback is systematically captured and translated into roadmap influence.

Without Service Experience, even a technically reliable service erodes customer trust through poor communication, inconsistent support, and unresolved recurring friction.

---

# 2. Target State (Level 3–4 Characteristics)

A strong service experience demonstrates:

- Clear support model with defined channels, ownership, and escalation paths published and known to customers
- Published response and resolution targets aligned to SLAs and OLAs
- High-quality knowledge base enabling self-service and reducing inbound support demand
- Efficient triage and routing; issues reach the right owner without bouncing
- Recurring issues reduced through root cause fixes rather than repeated manual resolution
- Timely, consistent stakeholder communication during disruptions from a single source of truth
- Customer feedback captured systematically and used to influence roadmap decisions

At higher maturity levels:

- Support demand is forecasted and proactively reduced before volume increases
- Automated resolution handles common request classes without engineer involvement
- Proactive outreach prevents avoidable escalations and improves satisfaction ahead of issues

---

# 3. Core Responsibilities

Service Experience requires the Engineering Manager or Service Owner to:

- Define and maintain the support operating model: who does what, when, and how
- Establish response and resolution targets aligned to SLAs and OLAs
- Ensure incident communications are timely, consistent, and reach the right audiences
- Maintain a knowledge base that is current, trusted, and enables self-service
- Ensure support issues are tracked, categorized, and used to drive engineering improvements
- Partner with support and customer teams to improve intake quality and triage effectiveness
- Reduce repeat issues by prioritizing root cause remediation over repeated manual handling

Ownership is measured by customer trust trends and repeat issue reduction, not ticket closure rate alone.

---

# 4. Governance Model & Cadence

Service Experience operates on a defined cadence.

**Weekly**
- Triage review: top tickets, escalations, and blockers
- Review repeat issues and high-impact customer friction points

**Monthly**
- Support metrics review: response times, resolution times, backlog health, and CSAT trends
- Knowledge base health review: gaps, outdated content, and deflection rate

**Quarterly**
- Customer experience review: top friction themes, roadmap implications, and self-service investment priorities

**Annually**
- Support model review: channels, ownership, and SLA alignment to service lifecycle stage
- Knowledge base strategy review aligned to customer segment evolution

Support governance that only activates at escalation is not a support model. It is crisis response.

---

# 5. Required Artifacts

The following artifacts must exist and remain current:

- Support model documentation covering channels, hours, ownership, and escalation paths
- Response and resolution targets with severity definitions
- Ticket taxonomy covering categories, severity, and root cause classification
- Status communication templates for incident updates and maintenance notices
- Knowledge base and self-service documentation
- Support-to-engineering handoff process
- Recurring issue register and problem backlog with prioritization

Artifacts must be discoverable by customers and support teams, not just by the engineering team.

---

# 6. Operating Mechanisms

Service Experience relies on structured mechanisms:

- Structured intake and triage: required fields, severity classification rules, and routing logic that gets issues to the right owner on first touch
- Communication mechanism: defined update cadence during incidents, single source of truth for status, and post-incident summaries
- Knowledge base lifecycle: docs-as-product ownership, update requirements triggered by incidents and major changes, regular content audits
- Repeat issue elimination: problem management backlog, prioritization of root cause work over repeated manual resolution, trend tracking
- Customer feedback loop: systematic collection, categorization, and translation of feedback into roadmap items

These mechanisms convert reactive support into a proactive trust-building system.

---

# 7. Key Metrics

Service Experience must be measurable.

Representative metrics include:

- First response time by severity
- Time to resolution by severity
- Ticket backlog age distribution
- Escalation rate and escalation aging
- CSAT and satisfaction trend over time
- Repeat issue rate: same root cause recurring across tickets
- Knowledge base usage and deflection rate: self-service success
- Percentage of tickets with complete intake information

Metrics should drive root cause investment and self-service improvement, not just SLA compliance tracking.

---

# 8. Leading Risk Indicators

Watch for:

- Customers unsure of how to get help or which channel to use
- Tickets missing actionable detail, causing engineering to spend time re-triaging
- Communication during incidents that stops after "we are looking into it"
- Same issues recurring across weeks or months with no root cause investment
- Knowledge base content that is outdated, untrusted, or not used by customers
- Support load silently consuming engineering capacity without appearing in planning
- Escalations that bypass process and create ad hoc response patterns

Support dysfunction is often invisible in engineering metrics while visible to every customer who experiences it.

---

# 9. Maturity Progression Model

Service Experience evolves through staged progression:

**Level 1: Reactive**
- Support channel and ownership defined
- Basic ticket tracking and response time measurement in place
- Baseline knowledge base created for top issues

**Level 2: Managed**
- Severity taxonomy and response targets implemented
- Communication standards and escalation paths established
- Repeat issue register created and root cause work begun

**Level 3: Optimized**
- Triage quality and routing efficiency measurably improved
- Self-service investment reducing inbound support demand
- Customer feedback integrated into roadmap planning
- Recurring issues systematically reduced through engineering fixes

**Level 4: Predictive**
- Support demand forecasted and proactively reduced
- Automated resolution in place for common request classes
- Proactive communications prevent avoidable escalations
- Satisfaction trends measured and continuously improved

Progression should be intentional and evidence-based.

---

# 10. Relationship to Other Domains

Service Experience depends on:

- Operational Reliability (customers experience reliability directly; SLA attainment is the foundation of support credibility)
- Engineering Excellence (operational readiness at launch prevents the support burden created by missing runbooks and unclear ownership)
- Observability & Intelligence (accurate detection and status communication during incidents requires instrumented systems with trustworthy signals)

Service Experience informs:

- Value Execution (recurring support issues surface unmet customer needs that belong in the backlog)
- Innovation & Modernization (high support load driven by legacy components is a retirement signal)
- Financial Stewardship (support cost is a TCO component that self-service investment can measurably reduce)

Without Service Experience, technical excellence does not translate into customer trust.

---

# Summary

Service Experience determines whether customers trust the service, not just whether the service works. Technical reliability is necessary but not sufficient. Durable customer relationships are built on predictable support, honest communication, self-service capability, and a consistent commitment to reducing the friction customers encounter over time.
