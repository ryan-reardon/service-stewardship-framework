# Value Execution

Value Execution ensures the service delivers measurable business outcomes predictably, efficiently, and sustainably.

It is the discipline of continuously validating:

- Are initiatives tied to explicit, measurable outcomes?
- Is prioritization driven by value and risk, not opinion or volume?
- Is delivery flow stable, predictable, and free of hidden drag?
- Are trade-offs visible and actively managed with stakeholders?

Value Execution transforms delivery from ticket throughput into outcome accountability.

---

# 1. Domain Purpose

To ensure that:

- Every initiative has a defined problem statement and success metric before work begins.
- Prioritization reflects business value and risk reduction, not urgency pressure.
- Delivery flow is stable, with manageable WIP and predictable cadence.
- Capacity is allocated honestly, including reliability, security, and modernization work.
- Learning loops — experiments, retrospectives, post-delivery measurement — are embedded in the system.

Without Value Execution, teams stay busy while outcomes remain uncertain.

---

# 2. Target State (Level 3–4 Characteristics)

A high-performing delivery system demonstrates:

- Initiatives have explicit, measurable outcome definitions before work starts
- Backlog is prioritized by business value and risk reduction, not opinion or recency
- Flow is stable: consistent delivery cadence, manageable WIP, low queue age
- Scope, cost, and schedule trade-offs are made explicit and documented
- Delivery includes reliability, security, and operability requirements as first-class constraints
- Learning loops are embedded: experiments, retrospectives, and post-delivery measurement
- Cross-team dependencies are tracked, owned, and actively managed

At higher maturity levels:

- Delivery forecasting is accurate and continuously updated
- Capacity planning is scenario-based, accounting for growth, incidents, and org changes
- Business value realization is measured post-delivery and feeds back into future prioritization
- Outcome data continuously refines what the team chooses to build next

---

# 3. Core Responsibilities

Value Execution requires the Engineering Manager or Service Owner to:

- Ensure every initiative has a problem statement, success metrics, acceptance criteria, and clear ownership
- Maintain backlog hygiene and prioritization discipline in partnership with Product and stakeholders
- Manage flow: limit WIP, remove bottlenecks, maintain queue health
- Make trade-offs explicit and documented — not silently absorbed
- Ensure delivery includes operational readiness: runbooks, monitoring, support enablement
- Manage cross-team dependencies transparently with owned risks and dates
- Protect capacity for reliability, security, and technical debt work — not just features
- Ensure retrospectives produce systemic, tracked improvements — not just discussion

Delivery ownership is measured by outcome realization, not ticket velocity.

---

# 4. Governance Model & Cadence

Value Execution operates on a defined cadence.

**Weekly**
- Delivery review: commitments, blockers, and dependency risks
- WIP and flow check: are we overcommitted?

**Bi-weekly / Sprint Cadence**
- Planning with explicit capacity assumptions
- Review and retrospective with action tracking and ownership

**Monthly**
- Flow metrics review: lead time, cycle time, throughput
- Portfolio health review: value delivered vs planned, variance causes

**Quarterly**
- Roadmap and capacity recalibration
- Dependency and risk review across services and teams

Governance cadence must match delivery rhythm to remain effective.

---

# 5. Required Artifacts

The following artifacts must exist and remain current:

- Outcome definition template applied to each initiative
- Prioritized backlog with value and risk scoring criteria
- Capacity allocation model (run / grow / modernize / security)
- Dependency map for major in-flight initiatives
- Delivery metrics dashboard (lead time, cycle time, throughput, predictability)
- Retrospective action log with owners, dates, and completion tracking

Artifacts should drive planning and review conversations, not sit in storage.

---

# 6. Operating Mechanisms

Value Execution relies on structured mechanisms:

- Business value scoring model (value, risk reduction, urgency, effort)
- Capacity guardrails with explicit allocations and no hidden work
- WIP limits and queue discipline enforced in planning
- Definition of Ready: outcome defined, dependencies identified, acceptance criteria clear
- Definition of Done: includes operability, security, and monitoring requirements
- Dependency management ritual with owners, dates, risks, and mitigation plans
- Post-delivery validation: did the shipped work achieve its stated outcome?

These mechanisms convert prioritization intent into delivery discipline.

---

# 7. Key Metrics

Value Execution must be measurable.

Representative metrics include:

- Lead time (idea to production)
- Cycle time (in-progress to done)
- Throughput (delivered items per sprint or month)
- Delivery predictability (percentage of commitments met)
- WIP and queue age health indicators
- Escaped scope rate (changes after commitment)
- Outcome realization rate (percentage of initiatives meeting success metrics)
- Dependency risk indicators (open dependencies, overdue handoffs)

Metrics should drive conversations about flow improvement, not just reporting.

---

# 8. Leading Risk Indicators

Watch for:

- Backlog with no scoring or visible prioritization rationale
- Commitments made without a capacity model
- Too many parallel initiatives moving slowly simultaneously
- Hidden work — support toil, incidents, unplanned requests — consuming untracked capacity
- Retrospectives held but producing no durable changes
- Work shipped without monitoring, runbooks, or support readiness
- Outcomes never measured after delivery

Delivery dysfunction accumulates gradually and is often normalized before it becomes visible.

---

# 9. Maturity Progression Model

Value Execution evolves through staged progression:

**Level 1: Reactive**
- Basic backlog and planning cadence established
- "Done" defined to include operational readiness
- Delivery metrics tracked, even if rough

**Level 2: Managed**
- Value-based prioritization and explicit scoring introduced
- WIP limits and dependency tracking implemented
- Retrospective action tracking formalized

**Level 3: Optimized**
- Flow metrics actively managed and bottlenecks targeted
- Capacity allocation guardrails enforced
- Outcome definitions required; post-delivery measurement in practice
- Trade-offs documented and reviewed with stakeholders

**Level 4: Predictive**
- Delivery forecasting accurate and continuously updated
- Scenario-based capacity planning in use
- Outcome data continuously refines prioritization
- Value realization measurement informs roadmap investment decisions

Progression should be intentional and evidence-based.

---

# 10. Relationship to Other Domains

Value Execution depends on:

- Strategic Stewardship (outcomes must trace to strategic priorities)
- Organizational Capability (delivery requires aligned ownership and capacity)
- Engineering Excellence (quality practices prevent rework that erodes predictability)
- Operational Reliability (reliability work must be explicitly included in capacity and prioritization)
- Architectural Integrity (architectural debt shapes delivery friction and must be a backlog citizen)

Value Execution also informs:

- Financial Stewardship (delivered outcomes justify investment)
- Innovation & Modernization (delivery capacity must protect modernization allocation)

Without Value Execution, strategic intent does not convert into measurable outcomes.

---

# Summary

Value Execution defines whether a team's effort produces outcomes or merely produces activity. Velocity without outcome accountability is misdirected energy. Durable delivery systems are built on disciplined prioritization, honest capacity models, stable flow, and a continuous commitment to measuring what was actually achieved.
